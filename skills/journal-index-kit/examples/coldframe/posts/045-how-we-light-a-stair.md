A stair is a sequence of edges, and in daylight each one casts a line. Get the lines right and the stair explains itself. Get them wrong and people look down for the whole flight.

At Lowther Yard the stair runs along a west wall under a roof light, so for most of the afternoon the sun rakes across it. We could not see it before the pour, so we modelled it: a sun path for the site, a simple massing of the stair, and a render for every hour from noon to six on the first day of each month.

## The nosing

The renders showed that a square nosing threw a hard black line across the tread below, which read as a step where there wasn’t one. A small chamfer, 6 mm at 45 degrees, softened the line into a grey band and put the darkest shadow exactly on the riser, where the eye expects the step to be.[^chamfer]

It is the same lesson as the bench, from the other side. An edge is not a detail you add at the end. It is how the object is read.

[^chamfer]: The contractor asked whether the chamfer could be dropped to save a day on the formwork. We showed them the four o’clock render and kept it.
