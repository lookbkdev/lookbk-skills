The first sample in our material library is a board of lime plaster, and the first piece in this series is about where it came from: a lime kiln at Harrow End that has been burning chalk, on and off, since 1840.

Lime plaster is slower than gypsum and more forgiving of old walls. It moves with them, lets damp out instead of trapping it, and hardens over months by taking carbon back from the air. The kiln’s owner described it as “a material that finishes itself”, which we have been quoting ever since.

We spent a morning watching the slaking: water poured onto quicklime, a cloud of steam, and a heat you can feel from three metres away. The putty that comes out of it has to rest for three months before it can go on a wall. Every lime plaster wall we specify now carries that quarter of a year in its programme, and every client has accepted it once they have touched a finished one.
