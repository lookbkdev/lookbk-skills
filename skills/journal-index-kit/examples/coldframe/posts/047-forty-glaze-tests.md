The brief for the Hollins kitchen asked for a splashback that “looks like the garden in October”. We took that literally, and then we took it to a kiln.

Forty tiles is not many for a glaze. It was enough to rule out the two we had assumed from the start: a tenmoku that read as brown in every light the kitchen gets, and a white that looked clinical next to the oak. The celadon was a late addition, mixed from the bottom of a bag of ash we had been keeping for something else.[^ash]

## What we tested

Each glaze went onto three clay bodies, a buff stoneware, a speckled one and a porcelain, at two thicknesses. That is where the forty comes from. We fired them together, on one shelf, so the only thing changing was the tile.

- The speckled body made every glaze look handmade, which the client loved on a tile and would have hated across three metres of wall.
- Porcelain made the celadon glassy and cold.
- The buff stoneware, at the thicker application, pooled green in the low spots and broke to brown on the edges. That is the garden in October.

The tiles are now in the material library on the back wall, labelled with the firing, the body and the date. The next person who asks for October will start where we finished.

[^ash]: Wood ash from the studio stove, sieved twice. Ash glazes are unpredictable from batch to batch, so we mixed enough for the whole kitchen in one go.
