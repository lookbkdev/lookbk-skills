A threshold is the edge of a room. It is the one place where two floors, two finishes and two ideas of the house meet, and it is usually resolved on site by whoever is holding the trim.

We have started drawing every threshold in a project at full size, on one sheet, before anything is ordered. There are usually more of them than anyone expects. The last house had twenty-three.[^count]

## What a threshold has to do

It has to take a step without making one. It has to stop the oak from meeting the stone in a line of grout that collects dust. It has to let a door swing over it and a draught not. And it has to do all this quietly, because a threshold that draws attention to itself makes a room feel like two rooms.

The best ones we have seen are a single piece of a third material: a strip of brass, a slip of slate, a length of oak laid across the grain. They admit the change instead of hiding it.

[^count]: Twenty-three, counting the cupboard under the stairs, which the client insisted on.
