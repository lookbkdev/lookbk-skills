Ruth Okafor-Lind has made most of the joinery in our projects since 2019. She trained in a boatyard, set up her own workshop in 1986, and has had eleven apprentices. We talked in her workshop on a wet Tuesday.

**What did the boatyard teach you that furniture didn’t?**

That nothing is square and nothing stays still. A boat moves all the time, so every joint has to allow for it. When I started making furniture I was surprised how much people expected wood to behave.

**You turn down a lot of walnut. Why?**

It is a lovely timber and it is everywhere at the moment, which is the problem. People ask for it because they have seen it, not because it is right for the room. Most of the time oak or ash will do the job better and age more honestly. I would rather have that conversation than make the wrong thing well.

**What do you look for in an apprentice?**

Patience with their own mistakes. Everyone makes them. The good ones look at the mistake for longer than they want to.

**And the edges? We have been writing about edges all year.**

I read them. You are right, mostly. But you could have asked me about the thumbnail profile before the second bench.
